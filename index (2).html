Research paper First draft December 2025, revised November 2026. 22 pages, in English.

Choice, Constraint, and Compliance: An Informal Economy Formalization Perspective

Abstract

This paper examines the informal economy through an incentive-based institutional framework. It argues that informality arises from two distinct logics: voluntary avoidance driven by cost–benefit calculations and compulsory informality resulting from structural constraints. Drawing on Equity Theory and the Collective Action Problem, it identifies six institutional drivers of informality and compares seven countries: Sweden, Germany, Italy, Estonia, Brazil, Argentina and Türkiye. The comparison suggests that credible penalties discourage evasion, while recurring tax amnesties encourage it. Digitalization appears to be the most effective tool, because it prevents evasion without relying on the individual taxpayer’s initiative. For Türkiye, the paper argues that high inflation makes under-reporting easier, that heavy reliance on consumption taxes shifts the burden onto salaried workers, and that ending recurring amnesties and expanding digitalization offer a more durable path to formalization.

Essay Written July 2026, revised November 2026. 4 pages, 18 numbered sources.

Is It Possible to Leave the Healthcare System to the Private Sector?

Can healthcare be left entirely to the market? The essay looks at what happened to US charity hospitals when donations fell during the Great Depression, how Taiwan built a hybrid system after 1995, and how overuse and administrative costs strain the system in Türkiye.
